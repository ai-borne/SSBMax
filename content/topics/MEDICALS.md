**The Medical Examination**

Once the Conference recommends you, the SSB process is not yet complete -- every recommended candidate proceeds to a medical examination at a designated military hospital, typically lasting 4-5 days. This examination checks whether you meet the physical and medical fitness standards required for commissioned service, and your final merit position is confirmed only after clearing it.

**Why Medicals Exist as a Separate, Final Gate**

The Conference assesses personality and leadership potential; it does not assess whether your body can sustain the physical demands of active service over decades -- combat training, high-altitude or extreme-climate postings, flying or diving duties depending on the service. Medicals exist specifically to verify that.

**What the Examination Covers**

- **General physical examination**: Height, weight, chest expansion, and overall build against service-specific minimums
- **Vision**: Uncorrected and corrected visual acuity (specific entries, like flying branches, require unaided vision close to 6/6); color vision testing
- **ENT (Ear, Nose, Throat)**: Hearing thresholds, nasal septum, and related checks
- **Dental examination**: Missing teeth, gum disease, and other dental fitness criteria
- **Pathology**: Blood tests, urine tests, and screening for underlying conditions
- **Radiology**: Chest X-ray and, where relevant, additional imaging
- **Cardiology**: ECG and cardiovascular fitness checks
- **Orthopedic examination**: Joint mobility, spine alignment, and screening for old injuries or deformities

**Common Grounds for Rejection**

- Uncorrected or correctable vision problems beyond the permitted limit for the entry scheme applied to
- Hearing defects
- Orthopedic issues -- old fractures, flat feet, knock knees, or restricted joint mobility
- Chronic conditions (certain skin conditions, asthma history, cardiac irregularities)
- Being significantly outside the height/weight/BMI band for your service and entry

**How to Prepare Well Before SSB**

- Get a general health check-up months in advance, not the week before -- correctable issues (minor dental work, treatable skin conditions) need lead time to resolve
- Maintain a consistent fitness and nutrition routine throughout your SSB preparation, not just in the days before medicals
- If you wear glasses, know your exact power and whether it falls within the limit for your target entry and branch
- Avoid last-minute crash diets or extreme measures to hit a weight target -- sudden changes can show up as flagged irregularities during examination
- Carry all relevant prior medical records (old injuries, existing prescriptions) rather than omitting them, since undisclosed conditions discovered later can affect your candidature more than a disclosed, explained one

**If You Are Referred for Review**

Some conditions result in a "temporary" rejection with a review medical board (RMB) or appeal medical board (AMB) option after a specified period or treatment -- this is not automatically final. Candidates who follow the prescribed treatment and reappear for a review board do sometimes get cleared; consult the specific process and timelines given by the examining hospital rather than assuming a first-round finding is the end of the process.

**Key Points to Remember**

- Medicals typically run 4-5 days at a designated military hospital after SSB recommendation
- This stage confirms physical fitness, not personality or leadership -- a strong SSB performance does not substitute for meeting medical standards
- Preparation should start months before your SSB date, not after receiving a recommendation
- A referral or temporary rejection is often reviewable -- understand the appeal process rather than assuming it is final
