# Preparing Your Body for the SSB Medical Examination

Unlike Psychology, GTO, and Interview, the medical examination cannot be influenced by how you present yourself in the moment — it measures fixed, verifiable facts about your body. That makes advance preparation the only lever you actually have.

## A Realistic Preparation Timeline

**6 months out:**
- Get a full baseline check-up: eyes, dental, general blood work, and a review of any past injury or chronic condition.
- Start (or continue) a consistent fitness routine targeting general cardiovascular health and a healthy weight range for your height, rather than a crash program.

**3 months out:**
- Address anything flagged in your baseline check — dental treatment, vision correction (within permitted limits for your target branch), or treatment for any lingering infection.
- Build toward the fitness standards typically required at SSB and later training (running, push-ups, sit-ups) — this also supports your performance in GTO's physical tasks.

**1 month out:**
- Stabilise your diet and sleep schedule rather than making sudden changes — the body's baseline readings (blood pressure, blood sugar, haemoglobin) are more reliable when your routine has been consistent for weeks, not days.
- Avoid starting any new intense workout programme this close to your medical, since acute muscle strain or unusual vitals from a sudden regime change can complicate readings.

**Final week:**
- Prioritise sleep and hydration.
- Avoid alcohol, and be mindful of any medication that could affect blood pressure or blood sugar readings on the day.
- Carry all existing prescriptions, spectacles, past treatment records, and identification documents.

## Fitness Fundamentals Worth Building Early

- **Cardiovascular endurance**: regular running or equivalent aerobic exercise, since several GTO outdoor tasks and later training both demand sustained stamina.
- **Core and functional strength**: push-ups, sit-ups, and basic bodyweight strength work support both the medical's general fitness assessment and GTO physical tasks like the Progressive Group Task.
- **Flexibility and joint health**: especially relevant if you have any old sports injury — untreated stiffness or minor old injuries can surface as orthopaedic findings.

## Nutrition Basics

- A balanced diet with adequate protein and iron supports healthy haemoglobin levels — low haemoglobin is one of the more common (and easily preventable) temporary rejection reasons.
- Avoid extreme, rapid weight-loss diets in the weeks before your medical; they can distort blood values and vitals in ways that raise unnecessary flags.
- Stay well-hydrated in the days leading up to the examination — some tests (urine analysis, certain blood parameters) are affected by hydration status.

## A Simple Weekly Fitness Structure

For candidates starting from a modest fitness base, a sustainable weekly pattern in the months before SSB might look like:

- **3 days/week**: running, building gradually from a comfortable distance toward the pace and distance expected for your target service's physical standards.
- **2–3 days/week**: bodyweight strength work — push-ups, sit-ups, squats, and basic core exercises, done in sets with adequate rest.
- **1 day/week**: a longer, easier session (a longer walk-run, a sport you enjoy) to build endurance without the intensity of dedicated training days.
- **1 rest day**: genuine recovery, since consistent progress depends on recovery as much as effort.

The specific numbers matter less than the pattern — steady, moderate effort sustained for months, rather than sporadic intense bursts.

## Weight Management Without Distorting Your Readings

If you need to adjust your weight to fall within the height-weight proportion expected, do it gradually over months through a sustainable calorie deficit or surplus combined with consistent exercise, not through short-term extreme measures. Rapid water-weight manipulation (severe dehydration-based "cuts," for instance) is particularly risky here, since several of the medical's own tests — blood pressure, certain blood values, urine analysis — are directly affected by hydration and can produce misleading or abnormal readings that create entirely new problems on examination day.

## What Not to Do

- Don't attempt to "cram" fitness in the final two weeks — sudden, unaccustomed exertion is more likely to cause an injury or an abnormal reading than to meaningfully change your fitness level.
- Don't hide a known condition hoping it won't be found — undisclosed history discovered during examination is treated as a credibility issue on top of the medical finding itself.
- Don't ignore minor recurring issues (persistent cough, occasional joint pain) assuming they'll resolve on their own — get them checked well in advance.

## Key Takeaway

The medical examination rewards **consistency over months**, not effort in the final days. Candidates who build steady fitness and health habits well before SSB rarely face surprises; those who try to fix everything in the last week almost always do.
