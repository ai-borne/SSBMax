# Group Planning Exercise Guide

The Group Planning Exercise (GPE) is an indoor GTO task, usually the first task on Day 3. Candidates are shown a large model/map of a fictional area and given a narrated problem — a natural disaster, accident, or crisis — with a fixed set of resources (vehicles, personnel, time, materials) to solve it. Unlike GD, GPE has a concrete right-and-wrong-shaped answer: a plan either respects the map's distances and resource limits or it doesn't.

## GPE Format and Structure

**Duration**: ~45 minutes total

**Step 1 — Narration (10 minutes)**: The GTO reads the problem aloud twice while candidates view the model. No notes are allowed during narration.

**Step 2 — Individual Solution (10 minutes)**: Each candidate writes their own plan on paper — routes, resource allocation, timings, and assumptions.

**Step 3 — Group Discussion (20-25 minutes)**: The group discusses individual solutions and must agree on one final combined plan, written by a designated candidate and read out to the GTO.

**Observation**: GTO watches both the individual writing (via the submitted sheet) and group behaviour during discussion — who listens, who dominates, who actually improves the plan.

## What the Problem Usually Involves

- A crisis situation (flood, fire, accident, epidemic, stranded group) at a specified location on the model
- A limited set of resources: vehicles with fixed capacity and speed, personnel counts, medical supplies, communication equipment
- Fixed time constraints (e.g. "help must arrive within 3 hours")
- Distances that must be read off the map's scale, not guessed
- Deliberately more demands than resources can fully cover, forcing prioritisation

## What GTO Assesses in GPE

**Reasoning & Organising Ability** (30%):
- Correct map reading (distances, routes, terrain features)
- Realistic resource allocation within stated limits
- Logical sequencing (what happens first, second, third)

**Practical/Administrative Sense** (25%):
- Assumptions are stated, not hidden
- Solution accounts for the time constraint, not just the immediate problem
- Contingency for likely complications (e.g. a route being blocked)

**Cooperation & Group Behaviour** (25%):
- Genuinely listening to alternative individual plans, not just defending your own
- Building a combined plan rather than a compromise nobody fully agrees with
- Contributing constructively, not going silent once your plan isn't picked

**Communication & Presentation** (20%):
- Individual sheet is legible, organised, and complete within 10 minutes
- Final group plan is presented clearly and confidently by whoever reads it out

## Individual Solution Strategy (10 minutes)

1. **Note the givens first** — resources, time limit, and locations, in a quick list, before drafting anything
2. **Read the map scale before estimating distances** — most lost marks come from misjudging distance, not from bad logic
3. **State assumptions on paper explicitly** — e.g. "Assuming the bridge is intact unless stated otherwise" — this is rewarded, silent assumptions are not
4. **Allocate resources against the highest-priority need first**, then work down — don't spread every resource thin trying to solve everything at once
5. **Write a simple timeline**: what departs/happens at t=0, t+15, t+30, etc., so the plan is checkable against the stated time limit
6. **Leave 1-2 minutes to reread** your own solution for an obvious resource-count or distance error

## Group Discussion Strategy (20-25 minutes)

**Opening**: Briefly let each candidate state their core approach (1 line each) before deep-diving into any one plan — this avoids the group anchoring on the first or loudest speaker.

**Merging, not voting**: The goal is a *combined* plan that takes the strongest elements of multiple individual solutions, not a majority vote for one candidate's untouched plan.

**Common contribution phrases**:
- "My solution used a similar route, but allocated the ambulance differently — can we compare timings?"
- "I think we're missing the time constraint here — if we send the truck first, does it still arrive within the 3 hours?"
- "Let's write the combined plan on the model as we agree on each piece, so we don't lose track."

**Defending your idea vs. accepting a better one**: If your individual plan gets set aside for a genuinely stronger group plan, say so and support it — GTO is watching for group orientation, not attachment to being "right."

## Common GPE Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Misreading the Map Scale
**Error**: Estimating distances by eye instead of using the given scale
**Impact**: Every downstream timing calculation becomes wrong
**Fix**: Always locate and use the scale bar first, before planning routes

### Mistake 2: Ignoring the Resource Limit
**Error**: Using more vehicles/personnel than the problem actually provided
**Impact**: Read as inattentive to detail — a basic administrative failure
**Fix**: List every resource given, with its capacity, before drafting the plan

### Mistake 3: Silent Assumptions
**Error**: Assuming favourable conditions (a road is clear, a bridge exists) without stating it
**Impact**: GTO cannot tell deliberate reasoning from a guess
**Fix**: Write "Assuming X" wherever the problem doesn't explicitly say

### Mistake 4: Dominating or Disengaging in Discussion
**Error**: Either pushing your own plan without listening, or going quiet once it isn't chosen
**Impact**: Both read as poor team orientation, the task's core assessment
**Fix**: Contribute to refining whichever plan the group is converging on

### Mistake 5: An Overambitious Plan
**Error**: Trying to fully solve every stated problem simultaneously with limited resources
**Impact**: Falls apart under a timing check; shows weak prioritisation
**Fix**: Solve the highest-priority need fully first, then address the rest with what remains

## GPE Preparation Strategy

**Map reading practice**: Practice reading distance/scale on any map or floor plan — this single skill fixes most avoidable GPE errors.

**Timed mock GPEs**: Simulate the 10/10/20-minute structure with a peer group using any resource-allocation scenario (not necessarily military) to build comfort with the time pressure.

**Mental math for logistics**: Practice quick distance ÷ speed = time calculations, since GPE plans are checked against a hard time limit.

**Study real disaster-response logic**: Reading about how emergency services actually prioritise (triage principles, evacuation order) builds intuition GTO scenarios are modelled on.

## Conclusion

GPE rewards candidates who read carefully, state their reasoning, and can let go of their own plan in favour of a better group one. It is one of the few GTO tasks with a partially objective "check" — the map and the resource limits — so basic accuracy matters as much as group behaviour.

**Success Formula**: Accurate Map Reading + Explicit Assumptions + Realistic Resource Allocation + Genuine Group Merging = GPE Excellence

**Next Step**: Carry the same resource-discipline mindset into Progressive Group Task (PGT), where planning is tested physically rather than on paper.
