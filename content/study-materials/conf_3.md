# Understanding SSB Results and Chest Numbers

## Why Chest Numbers, Not Names

From Day 1 onward, every candidate is identified only by a **chest number**, not by name. This is a deliberate anonymisation measure: it reduces bias between assessors and candidates, and it is also how results are declared — the President reads out chest numbers, not names, when announcing recommendations.

Write your chest number down and memorise it immediately; there is no announcement of "your" name at any point in the process.

## How Results Are Declared

After individual conferences finish, the board deliberates privately — candidates are not present for this discussion. Once deliberation concludes:

1. The entire batch reassembles, usually in the same hall or ground used for the conference.
2. The President (or an appointed officer) reads out the chest numbers of **recommended** candidates only.
3. If your chest number is called, you step forward; if it isn't, you have not been recommended this attempt.
4. Recommended candidates then proceed to the medical examination, usually starting the same day or the next.

There is no separate explanation of scores, marks, or a ranked list read aloud — SSB does not use a visible numeric score. The decision is binary: recommended or not recommended, for that particular attempt.

## What "Recommended" Actually Means

Being recommended by the board is **not** the final commissioning decision — it means you are cleared to proceed to the **medical examination**. Only after clearing medicals (and any subsequent merit-listing based on marks, depending on entry scheme) does actual selection get finalised. Recommended-but-medically-unfit candidates do not get commissioned unless they clear a medical review.

## If You Are Not Recommended

- No detailed feedback is given on the spot by most centres; a few provide general debrief sessions, but individualised scoring breakdowns are not shared.
- You are free to **reappear** for SSB in a future attempt through the same or a different entry scheme, subject to the eligibility rules of that scheme (age, attempt limits, qualifying exam cut-offs).
- Many officers currently serving were **not recommended on their first attempt** — a very large proportion of successful candidates need two or more attempts. A single non-recommendation is not a verdict on your overall potential.

## Practical Tips

- Note your chest number the moment it's assigned, and keep it visible/accessible through the conference — don't rely on memory alone under the stress of the moment.
- Don't try to interpret "signals" during the days leading up to the conference (which assessor smiled, how long your interview lasted); these are unreliable predictors and add needless anxiety.
- If recommended, be ready to move directly into the medical examination — pack and prepare for it in advance so you aren't caught off guard by the pace.
