# Using Your PIQ to Prepare for the Interview

Once you've submitted your PIQ, it becomes the single most important reference document for your personal interview. The smartest preparation you can do afterward is to treat your own PIQ as a study guide.

## Step 1: Re-Read Your PIQ as an Interviewer Would

After submission, get a copy or reconstruct what you wrote, and go through it line by line asking: *"If I were the IO, what would I ask about this entry?"*

- Every hobby, achievement, family detail, and academic data point is a potential question.
- Pay special attention to anything unusual, brief, or that could invite a follow-up — a gap year, a change of subject stream, a one-line hobby entry.

## Step 2: Prepare Honest, Detailed Answers for Every Entry

For each item on your PIQ, be ready to answer:

- **What exactly is this?** (e.g., what book, what specific role, what the activity actually involved)
- **Why does it matter to you?** (genuine motivation, not a rehearsed line)
- **What did you learn or how did it shape you?**

You don't need polished, quotable answers — you need answers that sound like **you**, because the IO is listening for authenticity and consistency, not eloquence.

## Step 3: Identify Your Own Inconsistencies Before the Board Does

Look for any place where your PIQ entries might seem to contradict each other or invite scrutiny:

- A stated career goal that doesn't obviously connect to your current academic stream — be ready to explain the connection in your own words.
- A family background section that might raise a natural question (e.g., no family service history despite applying for a defence career) — think through your own motivation independently of family influence.
- Achievements from several years ago with nothing more recent — be ready to explain what you've been focused on since.

## Step 4: Connect Your PIQ to "Why the Armed Forces"

The interview almost always circles back to your core motivation. Look at your own PIQ and ask honestly: does the picture it paints — your activities, your family background, your stated interests — genuinely support the answer you plan to give for why you want to join?

- If there's a mismatch, don't try to paper over it with a rehearsed answer — think through your actual motivation and be ready to explain it honestly, even if it doesn't perfectly match the "expected" profile.
- Genuine, self-aware answers consistently perform better than answers optimised to sound like an ideal candidate.

## Step 5: Practice Speaking About Your PIQ Out Loud

- Have a friend or family member ask you questions based on your own PIQ entries, cold, without you seeing the questions in advance.
- Notice where you hesitate or where your spoken answer differs meaningfully from what you wrote — those are the spots to think through further.
- The goal is not to memorise scripted answers, but to become genuinely comfortable and fluent discussing your own life, since that comfort is what actually reads as confidence in the interview room.

## Worked Example

Suppose your PIQ lists "photography" as a hobby with no further detail. A weak preparation stops there. A stronger preparation works through it like this:

- **What exactly is this?** — "I've been doing street and travel photography for about three years, mostly with my phone and a basic mirrorless camera."
- **Why does it matter to you?** — "It taught me to notice details and compose a scene quickly, which oddly helped in the PPDT — I found myself naturally structuring the story around a clear focal point."
- **What did you learn or how did it shape you?** — "It's made me more patient — a good photograph often means waiting for the right moment rather than forcing a shot."

Notice this isn't a rehearsed, dramatic story — it's a genuine, specific reflection that gives the IO something real to follow up on, and connects naturally to other parts of your SSB week without being forced.

## Building a Simple Self-Prep Table

For each PIQ entry, a short table like this — even done informally on paper — surfaces the gaps in your preparation quickly:

| PIQ Entry | Specific Detail I'll Give | Why It Matters to Me | Possible Follow-Up I Should Expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hobby: Photography | 3 years, street/travel, phone + mirrorless | Trains attention to detail and patience | "Show me one of your photos" / "What's your favourite subject?" |
| Family: Father — Government Service | 22 years, admin cadre | Saw discipline and service-orientation at home | "Did his career influence your decision?" |

Filling even five or six rows like this for your own PIQ will surface most of the genuine follow-up questions you're likely to face.

## Key Takeaway

Your PIQ is not a form you fill once and forget — it is the **script the interview is built around**. Candidates who go in having genuinely reflected on their own PIQ consistently perform better than those who wrote it quickly and never revisited it.
