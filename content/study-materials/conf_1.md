# Conference Day: Format and Procedure

The Conference is held on the morning of Day 5, after Psychology, GTO, and Interview are complete. It is not a fresh test — it is the board's final consolidation of everything they have already observed about you over the previous four days.

## Who Sits on the Board

- **President** — presides over the board and has the final say on recommendation
- **Deputy President** — assists the President, present at larger centres
- **Interviewing Officer (IO)** — has interviewed you one-on-one
- **Group Testing Officer (GTO)** — has observed you across all outdoor and indoor tasks
- **Psychologist** — has assessed your TAT, WAT, SRT, and SD responses

All assessors who evaluated you sit together in a semi-circle or U-shaped arrangement, with your chest number displayed so the board can identify you instantly.

## Sequence of Events

1. **Batch assembly** — your entire batch (usually 15–20 candidates) is called together and briefed on the process.
2. **Individual appearance** — candidates are called in one by one, in chest-number order. Each appearance lasts roughly 1–3 minutes.
3. **Brief interaction** — the President or another board member asks a small number of quick, direct questions (see the companion material on common conference questions).
4. **Dismissal** — you are asked to step out; the board does not deliberate on you in your presence.
5. **Board deliberation** — after all candidates have appeared, the assessors discuss each candidate privately, cross-referencing their independent observations.
6. **Result declaration** — the batch reassembles and the President reads out the chest numbers of recommended candidates.

## What Actually Happens Behind the Scenes

By the time you walk into the conference room, your fate is **already largely decided**. The IO, GTO, and Psychologist have each submitted independent written assessments before the conference begins. The conference itself serves three purposes:

- Give the President a first-hand look at every candidate, since the President has not interacted with most candidates individually before this point
- Let the board cross-check inconsistencies between what you told the IO, how you behaved in GTO tasks, and what your psychology responses suggested
- Provide a final, brief opportunity for any candidate to clarify something or make a closing impression

## Duration

A single conference for a full batch typically takes 2–4 hours: individual appearances take about 30–45 minutes in total, and board deliberation (which happens without candidates present) takes the rest of the morning. Results are usually declared by early-to-mid afternoon.

## The Room Itself

Most centres conduct the conference in a large hall or a dedicated conference room, distinct from where your interview took place. You will typically be called in from a waiting area just outside, so there is often a period of sitting with the rest of your batch before your own turn — this waiting period is a common source of unnecessary nerves, since candidates start reading into how long others' appearances took. Appearance length varies naturally by candidate and carries no reliable signal either way; don't try to interpret it.

## How the Board Uses Your PIQ and Records

Board members have your PIQ, your IO's interview notes, your Psychologist's write-up, and your GTO's task observations physically in front of them during your appearance. This is why conference questions often reference something specific you said days earlier — the board isn't relying on memory alone, it is actively cross-referencing the paper trail. Treat every earlier answer as something that could resurface here, which is one more reason consistency across the whole week matters more than any single strong moment.

## If You're Called Back a Second Time

At some centres, a candidate may occasionally be called back into the conference room a second time, often because the board wants a specific clarification after an initial round of deliberation. This is not inherently a bad sign — it can go either way — and reacting with panic or over-explaining is more likely to hurt you than the recall itself. Answer the specific question calmly and concisely, the same way you would the first time.

## Key Takeaway

Because the conference is a **review**, not a new evaluation, there is nothing to "perform" here. Your best strategy is to have been consistent and genuine across the previous four days — the conference simply surfaces whatever pattern the board has already built about you.
