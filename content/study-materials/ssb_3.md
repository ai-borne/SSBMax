# How to Prepare for SSB: A 90-Day Study Plan

SSB rewards steady, structured preparation far more than last-minute intensity — most of what it measures (personality patterns, physical fitness, genuine self-awareness) simply can't be crammed in the final week. This plan spreads preparation across three 30-day phases.

## Phase 1 (Days 1–30): Foundation

### Understand the Process Thoroughly
- Study the full five-day structure — screening, psychology, GTO, interview, conference — so nothing on the day itself is unfamiliar (see the companion "Complete Guide to the SSB Selection Process").
- Learn the 15 Officer Like Qualities and, honestly, assess where you currently stand on each — this becomes your personal development checklist for the next 90 days.

### Start Physical Conditioning
- Begin a basic running routine (start with 2–3 km, building gradually) plus push-ups and sit-ups — SSB's outdoor GTO tasks and the later medical both reward fitness built over months, not days.
- Establish a consistent sleep schedule now; it's one of the simplest, highest-leverage habits for both mental clarity in psychology tests and physical performance in GTO.

### Begin Current Affairs and General Awareness
- Start reading a newspaper or credible news source daily — not to memorise headlines, but to build genuine opinions you can discuss in the interview and Lecturette.
- Keep a simple running notes file of major national and defence-related developments; revisit it weekly rather than trying to absorb everything at once.

### Address Any Known Medical Issues Early
- If you already know of a vision, dental, or other correctable issue, this is the month to start treatment — see the medical preparation materials for the specific timeline that matters here (some issues genuinely need months, not weeks).

## Phase 2 (Days 31–60): Skill Building

### Psychology Test Practice
- Practise TAT, WAT, and SRT under **timed conditions** regularly (2–3 sessions a week), not to memorise "ideal" responses, but to get comfortable writing quickly and naturally under time pressure.
- After each practice session, review your own responses for genuine patterns — recurring themes, tone, and the qualities they reflect — rather than trying to force in "OLQ keywords."
- Practise the Self Description exercise honestly: write how you see yourself and, separately, how you believe your parents, teachers, and friends would describe you. Reconcile the two views honestly.

### GTO Task Familiarity
- Understand each GTO task type (GD, GPE, PGT, HGT, Command Task, FGT, Lecturette) conceptually — what it's testing and roughly how it's structured — so the format itself isn't a surprise.
- Practise structured group discussions with friends or a study group on a rotating set of current-affairs and abstract topics; focus on listening and building on others' points, not dominating.
- Prepare and rehearse 3–4 short Lecturette topics you could speak on confidently for a few minutes each, across different categories (social, defence, sports, technology).

### Interview Groundwork
- If you already have your PIQ format available (many candidates can access a sample well before their actual call-up), start filling a draft version and identify likely follow-up questions on every entry.
- Practise articulating your motivation for joining the Armed Forces in your own words — not a scripted line, but a genuine, examined answer you actually believe.

### Continue Fitness and Current Affairs
- Progress your running distance and strength routine steadily.
- Widen your current-affairs reading to include basic defence and geopolitical awareness relevant to India, since this commonly surfaces in both the interview and Lecturette.

## Phase 3 (Days 61–90): Consolidation and Mock Practice

### Full Mock Tests
- Take full-length, timed mock versions of OIR, PPDT, TAT, WAT, and SRT under conditions as close to the real thing as possible (no pausing, no reference material, strict timing).
- Do at least one full mock interview with someone unfamiliar with your background, so you experience answering PIQ-based questions cold rather than to a friend who already knows your story.

### Group Practice
- If possible, join or form a small peer group to practise GD and GPE-style discussions together — group dynamics are very difficult to simulate alone.
- Practise a Command Task scenario, even informally, to get comfortable briefing and leading a small group through a constrained problem.

### Fine-Tune Physical Fitness
- By this stage you should be running comfortably at a pace and distance appropriate for your target service's standards, with your push-up/sit-up counts stabilised — this final month is for maintenance and consistency, not sudden new intensity.

### Final PIQ and Interview Preparation
- Finalise your understanding of your own PIQ (see the companion material on using your PIQ to prepare for the interview) — you should be able to speak fluently and honestly about every entry.
- Review your current-affairs notes and re-test yourself on your prepared Lecturette topics.

### Logistics and Mental Readiness
- Confirm travel arrangements, required documents, and centre-reporting instructions well before your report date.
- In the final week, prioritise rest, sleep, and calm over any new preparation — SSB assesses natural, consistent behaviour, and arriving exhausted or over-drilled works against that goal.

## A Sample Weekly Rhythm (Phase 2–3)

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | Timed psychology practice (TAT/WAT/SRT) |
| Tue | Fitness (run + strength) + current affairs reading |
| Wed | Group discussion/GPE practice with peers |
| Thu | Fitness + PIQ/interview self-review |
| Fri | Lecturette practice + current affairs |
| Sat | Full mock test or mock interview |
| Sun | Rest, light review, honest self-assessment of the week |

## Principles That Matter More Than the Schedule

- **Consistency beats intensity.** A moderate, sustained effort across 90 days outperforms a frantic final two weeks, because SSB is built to detect exactly that kind of surface-level preparation.
- **Practise honestly, not performatively.** The goal of psychology and GTO practice is genuine self-understanding and comfort under pressure — not memorising a set of "correct" responses that won't hold up under the board's cross-checking.
- **Fitness and mental preparation run in parallel**, not sequentially — don't defer physical conditioning to "later," since it is the single area where preparation time has the most direct, measurable effect.
- **Adjust the plan to your starting point.** If you're already fit or already comfortable with public speaking, reallocate that time toward your weaker areas — this plan is a structure to adapt, not a rigid script.

Ninety days is enough time to build genuine readiness across every dimension SSB assesses — the candidates who benefit most from a plan like this are the ones who follow it consistently rather than intensely.
