# SSB Entry Schemes: NDA, CDS, TES, SSC and More

SSB is the common evaluation used across nearly every officer-entry route into the Army, Navy, and Air Force — but the eligibility, age bracket, and academic requirement to *reach* the SSB stage differs significantly by scheme. Choosing the right entry point matters as much as your SSB preparation itself.

## National Defence Academy (NDA)

- **Who it's for**: candidates straight out of Class 12, before graduation.
- **Age**: roughly 16.5–19.5 years at the time of joining (exact bracket set each notification cycle).
- **Academic requirement**: Class 12 pass; Physics and Mathematics required for Army wing eligibility for certain streams, and for Navy/Air Force wings.
- **Route**: written exam (conducted by UPSC) → SSB → medical → three years at NDA (tri-service) followed by service-specific pre-commission training.
- **Why candidates choose it**: the longest training runway and the classic route for those certain of a defence career straight after school.

## Combined Defence Services (CDS)

- **Who it's for**: graduates.
- **Age**: broadly 19–24 years for IMA (Army)/INA (Navy)/AFA (Air Force), and 19–25 years for OTA (Officers Training Academy, Short Service Commission).
- **Academic requirement**: a graduate degree (any stream for IMA/OTA; an engineering degree for INA and Air Force technical entries; PCM at 10+2 plus a B.E./B.Tech is required for certain AFA routes).
- **Route**: written exam (UPSC) → SSB → medical → training at IMA, INA, AFA, or OTA depending on the stream selected.
- **Why candidates choose it**: the standard route for graduates who did not go through NDA.

## Technical Entry Scheme (TES)

- **Who it's for**: Class 12 pass candidates (PCM) heading straight into a technical/engineering commission in the Army.
- **Academic requirement**: minimum aggregate (commonly around 60%) in Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics at 10+2, along with a qualifying JEE (Main) score used for shortlisting.
- **Route**: shortlisting based on JEE (Main) score → SSB → medical → integrated engineering training leading to a technical commission, with **no separate written exam** conducted by the Army itself.
- **Why candidates choose it**: an early, direct path into technical arms for students strong in PCM who don't want to wait for a separate defence written exam.

## Air Force Common Admission Test (AFCAT)

- **Who it's for**: graduates seeking a Flying Branch, Ground Duty (Technical), or Ground Duty (Non-Technical) commission in the Air Force.
- **Route**: AFCAT written exam (conducted directly by the IAF, twice a year) → SSB → medical.
- **Why candidates choose it**: the primary graduate-entry route into the Air Force outside CDS.

## Short Service Commission (SSC)

- **What it is**: a non-permanent commission (typically an initial tenure of around 10 years, extendable), available across many of the entry routes above (CDS-OTA, AFCAT SSC streams, Navy SSC, and dedicated women's SSC entries).
- **Why candidates choose it**: a shorter service commitment compared to Permanent Commission routes, and historically the primary entry route for women officers, though eligibility for Permanent Commission has expanded over time across services.

## University Entry Scheme / Direct Entries

Some technical and specialist roles (engineering graduates, law graduates for the Judge Advocate General branch, and similar) have dedicated entry routes with their own shortlisting criteria, followed by the same SSB and medical process.

## Permanent Commission vs. Short Service Commission

This distinction is worth understanding independently of which written exam gets you to SSB:

- **Permanent Commission (PC)**: service until the standard retirement age/rank-based tenure, with a defined long-term career path, pension eligibility, and typically a longer initial training duration.
- **Short Service Commission (SSC)**: a fixed initial tenure (commonly around 10 years, often extendable), after which an officer may leave service, apply to be considered for a Permanent Commission (where such conversion is offered, subject to vacancies and performance), or continue on extension depending on the applicable policy at the time.

Some entry schemes (like NDA) lead to Permanent Commission by design; others (like many OTA and AFCAT SSC streams) start as Short Service Commission with conversion possibilities that vary by service and change periodically — always verify the current policy for your specific entry rather than relying on older information, since these terms are revised from time to time.

## A Quick Comparison Table

| Scheme | Typical Age Band | Academic Requirement | Written Exam | Leads To |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NDA | ~16.5–19.5 yrs | Class 12 pass (PCM for certain streams) | UPSC NDA exam | 3-year tri-service academy, then service-specific training |
| CDS (IMA/INA/AFA) | ~19–24 yrs | Graduate (stream varies by wing) | UPSC CDS exam | Service-specific pre-commission academy |
| CDS (OTA) | ~19–25 yrs | Graduate (any stream) | UPSC CDS exam | OTA, Short Service Commission |
| TES | Class 12 pass, PCM | 10+2 with min. aggregate in PCM | JEE (Main) score-based shortlisting | Integrated technical/engineering training, Army |
| AFCAT | Graduate | Stream varies by branch | AFCAT (IAF-conducted) | Air Force Academy, Flying/Technical/Non-Technical branches |

Exact age brackets, percentage cut-offs, and academic stream requirements are set and revised in each notification cycle — treat the ranges above as a general orientation, and always confirm current figures against the relevant service's official notification before applying.

## The Common Thread

Regardless of entry scheme, the sequence is always: **eligibility/written exam or shortlisting → SSB → medical examination → training**. SSB itself does not differ in format based on your entry scheme — the same five-day process, the same OLQ framework, and the same three assessors apply whether you're 17 and applying via NDA or 23 and applying via CDS. What differs is the age bracket, the academic prerequisite to reach the SSB stage, and the length/type of training and commission that follows.

## Choosing the Right Scheme for You

- **Still in school (up to Class 12)**: NDA or TES are your earliest entry points — NDA if you want the broadest options across services, TES if you're set on a technical Army commission and strong in PCM.
- **Already a graduate or finishing graduation**: CDS or AFCAT, depending on which service and branch interests you.
- **Specific technical/professional background** (engineering, law, medicine): check for a dedicated direct-entry scheme matching your qualification before defaulting to the general graduate routes.

Whichever route gets you there, the SSB preparation itself — the focus of the rest of this material — is identical in substance.
