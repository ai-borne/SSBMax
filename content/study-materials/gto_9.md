# Final Group Task Strategy

The Final Group Task (FGT) is the last outdoor obstacle task of the GTO series, typically run immediately after the Snake Race / Group Obstacle Race. Structurally it resembles the Progressive Group Task (PGT) and Half Group Task (HGT) — the whole group must move itself and its helping materials across a physical obstacle without touching the ground, water, or out-of-bounds zones — but it carries the specific weight of being the task GTO uses to judge whether your teamwork and energy are consistent, not just present at the start of the day.

## FGT Format and Structure

**Duration**: 20-30 minutes

**Group Size**: Full group, 8-10 candidates

**Structure**: One or more physical obstacles, using ropes, planks, and other helping materials, that the whole group and its equipment must cross under the same rules as PGT/HGT

**Timing in the day**: Last outdoor task, usually following the Snake Race — candidates are already physically tired from the day's earlier tasks

**Observation**: GTO watches the same criteria as PGT/HGT, plus a specific interest in whether behaviour holds up under fatigue

## Why FGT Matters More Than Its Difficulty Suggests

FGT is rarely the hardest single obstacle of the three days — PGT and Snake Race are often physically tougher. What makes it significant is timing: it is the last data point GTO collects on your group behaviour, coming after roughly two full days of GD, GPE, PGT, HGT, Lecturette, Individual Obstacles, Command Task, and the Snake Race. GTO's overall impression is formed across all of these tasks together, and FGT is the closing chapter of that pattern.

Two candidates who perform identically on FGT itself can still be assessed differently based on the trend: one who has been consistently cooperative and is now visibly tired but still helping others reads very differently from one who was disengaged all along and is now also tired.

## What GTO Assesses in FGT

**Physical Stamina & Determination** (25%):
- Continuing to contribute despite two days of physical tasks
- Not visibly giving up or checking out when tired

**Teamwork & Cooperation** (30%):
- Helping teammates without being asked, same as every earlier obstacle task
- Sharing the physical load rather than letting the same few members carry it

**Consistency of Behaviour** (25%):
- Applying the same safety discipline and rule awareness as in PGT/HGT (no touching restricted zones, materials not thrown carelessly)
- Not abandoning earlier good habits (spontaneous helping, clear communication) just because it's the last task

**Positive Attitude Under Fatigue** (20%):
- Maintaining energy and encouragement for the group, not visible frustration or blame
- Treating FGT as worth genuine effort rather than "coasting to the finish"

## FGT Preparation Strategy

**Physical conditioning across the full week, not just task-specific practice**: Since FGT tests stamina late in a demanding sequence, general fitness (stamina runs, basic strength work) matters more here than any FGT-specific drill.

**Mental rehearsal of PGT/HGT fundamentals**: Since the obstacle mechanics mirror earlier tasks, review the same core rules — no touching the ground/water/out-of-bounds, materials must cross with the group, rule violations restart from the obstacle's start point.

**Practice sustaining cooperative behaviour when tired**: In mock GTO sessions, deliberately run the group-task portion after some physical exertion (a short run, a few PGT-style obstacles) so you experience contributing while fatigued before the real assessment day.

**Review your own pattern across the three days**: If you know you tend to go quiet under pressure or to dominate early and fade, use FGT specifically as the task where you correct that — GTO's final impression weighs recent behaviour heavily.

## Common FGT Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Treating It as "Just One More Obstacle"
**Error**: Mentally checking out because it feels repetitive after PGT, HGT, and Snake Race
**Impact**: A flat or disengaged final task can undercut an otherwise strong three-day pattern
**Fix**: Treat FGT with the same energy as the first task of the day — GTO is watching the trend, not giving credit for effort already spent

### Mistake 2: Letting Fatigue Excuse Poor Cooperation
**Error**: Withdrawing from helping others because you personally are tired
**Impact**: Reads as self-focused exactly when teamwork matters most
**Fix**: Contribute in smaller ways if full physical effort isn't possible — spotting, guiding, encouraging still count as participation

### Mistake 3: Repeating Earlier Rule Mistakes
**Error**: Rushing and touching a restricted zone or mishandling helping materials, as might have happened in PGT
**Impact**: Signals no learning across the three days
**Fix**: Apply the same careful rule-awareness from your best earlier task, not your worst

### Mistake 4: Racing to Finish Instead of Finishing as a Team
**Error**: A few strong members finishing the obstacle while others lag behind unhelped
**Impact**: GTO is assessing the group, not individual speed
**Fix**: The task isn't complete, in GTO's eyes, until the whole group and all materials are across

## FGT Day Tips

**Before FGT**:
- Hydrate and, if a short break is available, use it fully — physical recovery matters more here than mental preparation
- Reset mentally: treat it as a fresh task, not the tail end of a long day

**During FGT**:
- Contribute early — a strong, visible start signals sustained effort, not just a late push
- Watch for teammates who are struggling more than you and offer help proactively
- Maintain clear, calm communication even if the group is tired or frustrated

**After FGT**:
- This is typically the last outdoor GTO task — there's little to gain from replaying it mentally; shift focus to whatever comes next (Lecturette review, Command Task, or moving toward Day 5)

## Conclusion

FGT rarely requires new skills beyond what PGT and HGT already taught — its real test is whether your teamwork, rule discipline, and positive attitude survive two days of physical and mental fatigue. Treat it as the closing proof of the pattern GTO has been building since Group Discussion on Day 3, not as a separate challenge.

**Success Formula**: Sustained Stamina + Consistent Cooperation + Rule Discipline + Positive Energy Under Fatigue = FGT Excellence

**Remember**: GTO's final impression is a pattern across all nine tasks, not a single obstacle — finish the way you started.
