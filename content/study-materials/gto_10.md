# Individual Obstacles Test (IOT) Guide

Individual Obstacles Test is the one GTO task scored purely on individual physical performance rather than group behaviour. Candidates attempt a course of 10 outdoor obstacles of increasing difficulty, one at a time, within a fixed time limit. Each obstacle cleared successfully earns fixed points; there is no team to coordinate and no plan to negotiate — only technique, fitness, and composure under a ticking clock.

## IOT Format and Structure

**Duration**: Roughly 3-4 minutes per candidate

**Obstacles**: 10 standard outdoor obstacles, arranged in increasing difficulty

**Attempt Order**: Candidates generally choose their own sequence within the course

**Scoring**: Fixed points per obstacle, awarded only on correct, safe completion

**Supervision**: GTO and assistants observe technique and safety at each obstacle

**Repeat Attempts**: Usually one attempt per obstacle within the time window

## What Makes IOT Different from Other GTO Tasks

**IOT vs Group Tasks (PGT/HGT/Snake Race)**:

| Aspect | Group Tasks | IOT |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| Unit of Assessment | Group + individual role | Individual only |
| Skill Tested | Leadership, cooperation, planning | Physical fitness, technique, composure |
| Helping Materials | Planks, ropes, drums | None — obstacles are fixed structures |
| Failure Consequence | Group setback | Personal point loss only |
| Time Pressure | Shared across group | Personal, strict per-candidate window |

**Critical Insight**: IOT cannot be talked through. No amount of ideas or communication skill substitutes for practiced physical execution here.

## The 10 Obstacles

While exact obstacle names and layout vary by centre, IOT courses are drawn from a standard set of physical challenges:

- **Balancing Structures**: Narrow beams or ropes crossed without falling
- **Wall/Vertical Obstacles**: Climbing over walls of varying height
- **Long Jump / Ditch Crossing**: Clearing a horizontal gap
- **Tarzan Swing / Rope Obstacles**: Using a rope to cross a gap or descend
- **Net Climbing**: Scaling a cargo-style net structure
- **Tunnel / Crawl Obstacles**: Moving through a low, confined space quickly
- **High Jump Bar**: Clearing a raised bar without dislodging it
- **Burma Bridge / Rope Bridge**: Crossing on suspended rope supports
- **Commando Crawl**: Crawling under an obstacle close to the ground
- **Final Sprint/Jump**: A concluding obstacle combining speed and one physical skill

**Note**: Specific obstacles and order differ across boards; the underlying skills (balance, climbing, jumping, crawling, upper-body strength) stay consistent.

## IOT Process and Approach

### Phase 1: Course Briefing (1-2 minutes)
**What Happens**:
- GTO explains obstacle sequence and any rules for that centre
- Points value per obstacle may be indicated
- Safety instructions given for higher-risk obstacles

**Your Focus**:
- Note which obstacles look attemptable versus risky for you
- Mentally sequence your approach if choice is allowed
- Ask for clarification on any obstacle you don't understand

### Phase 2: Attempt Window (3-4 minutes)
**What Happens**:
- You move through the course attempting obstacles in the fixed time
- GTO/assistants watch technique and award points per clearance
- Clock runs continuously across all obstacles

**Your Actions**:
- Start with obstacles you're confident about to bank points early
- Commit fully to each attempt — hesitant, half-effort attempts fail more often
- Move on immediately after a clear success or a decided skip; don't linger

**Time Discipline**: Every second spent deciding is a second not spent attempting.

### Phase 3: Completion (Remaining time)
**What Happens**:
- Time expires or all obstacles attempted
- GTO notes final tally and observed technique/safety

**Your Behavior**:
- Finish the attempt you're on safely rather than rushing the very last seconds
- Maintain composure regardless of how many obstacles you cleared

## Effective IOT Strategies

### Strategy 1: Bank Easy Points First
**Sequence Wisely**:
- Attempt obstacles you're most confident about early
- Build momentum and confidence before harder obstacles
- Avoid spending your best energy on the hardest obstacle first if choice is allowed

### Strategy 2: Decide, Don't Deliberate
**Fast Risk Assessment**:
- Look at an obstacle for 2-3 seconds and decide: attempt or skip
- A skipped obstacle costs only its points; a failed, unsafe attempt can cost time and injury risk
- "I can clear this cleanly" vs "I'm not confident" — commit to the answer immediately

### Strategy 3: Technique Over Force
**Efficient Movement**:
- Use proper form for climbing, jumping, and crawling rather than raw effort alone
- Controlled momentum clears obstacles more reliably than a rushed lunge
- Practised technique reduces both failure rate and injury risk

### Strategy 4: Composure Under the Clock
**Mental Control**:
- A missed obstacle should not affect performance on the next one
- Treat each obstacle as an independent attempt
- Visible frustration or panic is itself a negative signal to the GTO

### Strategy 5: Safety First, Always
**Non-Negotiable**:
- Never attempt an obstacle in a way that risks a fall from height or a bad landing
- A cautious, controlled clearance beats a reckless one that risks injury
- GTO values sensible risk-taking, not recklessness

## What GTO Assesses in IOT

**Obstacles Cleared** (50%):
- Raw count and difficulty of obstacles successfully completed

**Technique and Control** (25%):
- Form, balance, and control during each attempt
- Efficient rather than wasteful movement

**Composure and Decision-Making** (15%):
- Quick, sensible attempt-or-skip decisions
- Staying steady after a failed attempt

**Safety Awareness** (10%):
- No reckless or unsafe attempts
- Sensible judgment about personal limits

## IOT Performance Indicators

**Excellent Performance**:
- Cleared most or all obstacles with clean technique
- Quick, confident decisions on each obstacle
- No unsafe attempts
- Maintained composure throughout

**Good Performance**:
- Cleared a majority of obstacles
- Mostly efficient technique with minor hesitation
- Recovered composure quickly after any miss

**Average Performance**:
- Cleared roughly half the obstacles
- Some hesitation or wasted time deciding
- Occasional loss of composure after a miss

**Weak Performance**:
- Cleared very few obstacles
- Visible panic, rushed or unsafe attempts
- Time lost to indecision rather than attempts

## IOT Preparation Strategy

**Physical Readiness**:
- Running for cardiovascular endurance
- Upper body and grip strength for climbing and rope obstacles
- Core strength and balance training for beam/bridge obstacles
- Practice controlled jumping and landing technique

**Mental Readiness**:
- Build comfort with quick attempt-or-skip decisions under time pressure
- Practice staying composed immediately after a failed attempt
- Visualize the course as a series of independent attempts, not one continuous test

**Practice Approach**:
- Train on an obstacle course if accessible, or simulate individual elements (wall climbs, balance beams, crawling under low bars)
- Time yourself on short circuits to build comfort with the clock
- Focus conditioning on functional fitness over pure gym strength

## IOT Day Tips

**Before IOT**:
- Warm up thoroughly — this is a physical test with real injury risk if muscles are cold
- Positive, focused mindset: "One obstacle at a time"
- Wear appropriate footwear and clothing for movement

**During IOT**:
- Decide fast, commit fully, move on immediately
- Use proper technique over raw force
- Stay composed after any miss
- Never risk an unsafe attempt for extra points

**After IOT**:
- Don't dwell on missed obstacles going into the next GTO task
- Stay physically loose for tasks later the same day

## Self-Assessment After IOT

**Critical Questions**:
- Did I clear obstacles I had practiced and was confident about?
- Did I make fast, sensible attempt-or-skip decisions instead of hesitating?
- Did I stay composed after any failed attempt?
- Did I avoid any unsafe or reckless attempt?

**Honest Evaluation**:
- Identify which obstacle types need more physical preparation
- Note whether hesitation or physical limitation cost more points
- Carry composure lessons into the remaining GTO tasks

## Conclusion

Individual Obstacles Test is a straightforward physical assessment inside an otherwise group-heavy GTO series: fitness, technique, and quick decision-making under a strict clock. Excel in IOT through consistent physical conditioning, decisive attempt-or-skip judgment, controlled technique, and composure that carries you cleanly into the next task.

**Success Formula**: Physical Conditioning + Fast Decisions + Controlled Technique + Composure + Safety = IOT Success

**Remember**: IOT is the one GTO task where preparation is almost entirely in your own hands — daily fitness discipline in the weeks before SSB pays off directly here.

**Next Step**: Carry the same composure and decisiveness into Command Task, where you lead others through a similar physical obstacle.
